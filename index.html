How to Use the GPA Calculator

The GPA Calculator computes your current GPA on the standard 4.0 scale by weighting each course grade by its credit hours. It also calculates what final exam grade you need to reach a target GPA, and tracks your cumulative GPA across multiple semesters.

Add your courses, select the letter grade (A+/A/A−/B+/B/B− etc.) and enter the credit hours for each. The GPA updates in real time as you add courses, showing the weighted average and the letter grade equivalent.

A nuance to understand: different institutions use different GPA scales. Most US universities use a 4.0 scale where A=4.0, B=3.0, C=2.0, D=1.0. Some schools use +/- grades (A+=4.3 or 4.0, A=4.0, A-=3.7). Check your institution's specific scale, as it affects whether a C+ is 2.3 or simply 2.0.

📊 Worked Example

Semester with 5 courses:

  • Calculus (4 credits) A = 4.0 → 16 quality points
  • English (3 credits) B+ = 3.3 → 9.9 quality points
  • History (3 credits) B = 3.0 → 9.0 quality points
  • Chemistry (4 credits) C+ = 2.3 → 9.2 quality points
  • Semester GPA: 44.1 ÷ 14 credits = 3.15 GPA

Frequently Asked Questions

How is GPA calculated?

GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credit Hours. Quality points for each course = Grade Points × Credit Hours. Example: An A (4.0) in a 3-credit course gives 12 quality points. A B (3.0) in a 4-credit course gives 12 quality points. Sum all quality points, divide by total credit hours.

What GPA do I need for graduate school?

Most graduate programs look for a 3.0+ GPA minimum. Competitive programs (MBA, law, medical) often want 3.5+. Some PhD programs accept 3.0–3.2 with strong research experience. GPA is considered alongside GRE/GMAT scores, personal statement, and recommendations — a lower GPA can sometimes be offset by other strengths.

What is the difference between semester GPA and cumulative GPA?

Semester GPA is calculated only from courses in that term. Cumulative GPA is weighted average across all semesters, using all credits and all quality points earned throughout your academic career. Graduate schools and employers typically look at your cumulative GPA, though a strong upward trend (improving GPA over time) is also noticed favorably.

Can I retake a course to improve my GPA?

Many institutions have a grade forgiveness or grade replacement policy that allows you to retake a course and have only the new grade count. Rules vary: some replace the original; others average both; some show both on the transcript but count only the higher grade in GPA. Always check your institution's specific policy before retaking courses.

What GPA is needed to graduate with honors?

Typical honors thresholds at US universities: Cum Laude (with praise): 3.5 GPA, Magna Cum Laude (with great praise): 3.7 GPA, Summa Cum Laude (with highest praise): 3.9+ GPA. These vary by institution — some rank by percentile rather than fixed GPA cutoffs. Latin honors are typically calculated on cumulative final GPA.
